### Detailed Analysis of Each Candidate

#### 1. CL.CP.Art.412 — Calumnia (Código Penal)

**Verbatim Text (BCN):** "Art. 412. Es calumnia la imputación de un delito determinado pero falso y que pueda actualmente perseguirse de oficio."

**Agent-Fit:** Open to any person. No special agent class required.

**Applicability to CL-001:**
The violation title is "False Accusation of Aggression (Calumnia) by LATAM [[SPK-stewardess-accuser]]". The stewardess accused the passenger of aggression — a specific, determinate fact constituting a crime (lesiones or similar). This accusation was false, as confirmed by PDI's CCTV review. The violation JSON includes segment `I-002_05_NAR-07_STG_7_post_removal_investigation.seg-51`, where the PDI official states: **"Pero en si una agresion a una persona....no hay"** (But in itself, an aggression against a person... there isn't one). Segment `seg-43` states: **"En este caso, por ese lado, lesiones no hay"** (In this case, on that side, there are no injuries). The passenger himself states in segment `I-002_17_NAR-21_STG_29.seg-93`: **"no hubo una agresión, hubo una acusación que fue comprobada que fue mentira"** (there was no aggression, there was an accusation that was proven to be a lie).

**Key Element — "que pueda actualmente perseguirse de oficio":** The false imputation of aggression (a crime against persons) is prosecutable ex officio. The accusation was made to PDI, a law enforcement authority, which satisfies the requirement of imputation before authority.

**Critical Open Question (OQ-001-DOLO-AZAFATA):** The violation's open question asks: "¿Actuó la azafata con conocimiento de falsedad (dolo) o error?" For calumnia, the imputation must be false, but the perpetrator must also act with knowledge of the falsity or malice. The violation JSON notes this blocks the `conocimiento_falsedad` element. This is a critical evidentiary gap: if the stewardess genuinely believed there was aggression, calumnia may not be established, and the claim would shift to negligence-based civil liability.

**Verdict:** **Correct provision.** Applicable if the stewardess acted with knowledge of the falsity or malice (dolo). Requires factual confirmation of the subjective element.

#### 2. CL.CP.Art.211 — Denuncia Calumniosa (Código Penal)

**Verbatim Text (BCN):** "Art. 211. El que maliciosamente presentare una denuncia por la cual se impute falsamente a otra persona un hecho determinado constitutivo de delito, infracción administrativa o infracción disciplinaria será sancionado..."

**Agent-Fit:** Open to any person. No special agent class required.

**Applicability to CL-001:**
This article is **more specific** than Art. 412 in one important respect: it covers the **act of filing a denuncia** (complaint) before the competent authority. The PDI officer's coaching at segment `I-002_05_NAR-07_STG_7_post_removal_investigation.seg-58` — **"Por ese motivo podemos tomarte una denuncia pero es como tu persona, no como LATAM"** (For that reason we can take a denuncia from you, but as yourself personally, not as LATAM) — directly implicates the denuncia pathway. If the stewardess filed or intended to file a denuncia based on the false aggression accusation, Art. 211 is engaged.

**Concurrency with Art. 412:** The violation's open question `OQ-CL001-9.3-CP211-CONCURRENCIA` asks: "¿Aplica CP Art. 211 concurrentemente con Art. 412 (non bis in idem)?" In Chilean doctrine, Art. 211 is a special provision for the specific act of filing a false denuncia, while Art. 412 is the general calumnia provision. They can concur, but the non bis in idem principle may limit double punishment for the same underlying conduct. The more specific provision (Art. 211) typically prevails when the conduct consists of filing a denuncia.

**Key Element — "maliciosamente":** The same subjective element issue applies: the denuncia must be filed maliciously, with knowledge of its falsity.

**Verdict:** **Correct provision.** Particularly apt if a formal denuncia was filed. The coaching sequence (seg-58) strengthens the nexus to this article.

#### 3. CL.CC.Art.2314 — Responsabilidad Extracontractual (Código Civil)

**Verbatim Text (BCN):** "Art. 2314. El que ha cometido un delito o cuasidelito que ha inferido daño a otro, es obligado a la indemnización; sin perjuicio de la pena que le impongan las leyes por el delito o cuasidelito."

**Agent-Fit:** Open to any person (natural or juridical).

**Applicability to CL-001:**
This is the **civil-law anchor** for damages. The passenger suffered concrete harm: missed flight, reputational damage from being treated as a disruptive passenger, and emotional distress. The violation JSON includes the passenger's own words at `I-002_17_NAR-21_STG_29.seg-32`: **"me hicieron dos difamaciones que fueron comprobadas por la pdi que no pasaron"** (they made two defamations against me that were proven by PDI not to have happened).

**Liability Chain:**
- **LATAM** as employer would be vicariously liable under Art. 2320 CC for the acts of its personnel (the stewardess who made the false accusation, and the supervisor who maintained the false information). Segment `I-002_18_NAR_LATAM_STG_2.seg-13` shows the airline supervisor stating: **"Señor, esa es la información que nosotros tenemos De lo sucedido, no podemos cambiar La información que tenemos..."** (Sir, that is the information we have about what happened, we cannot change the information we have...). This refusal to correct the record after PDI's exoneration is a continuing act causing harm.
- **The State** (PDI) could be liable under Ley 18.575 Art. 42 for falta de servicio if the PDI officer's omission of the exoneration from the parte constitutes deficient service.

**Open Question (OQ-CL001-9.3-CC2331-DANO-MORAL):** "¿Limita CC Art. 2331 el daño moral por imputaciones injuriosas?" Art. 2331 CC provides that in cases of injury (injuria), moral damages may be limited. This could cap the recoverable moral damages if the court characterizes the harm as flowing from an injurious imputation rather than a general tort.

**Verdict:** **Correct provision.** Essential for the civil damages dimension. Requires proof of the harmful act, the damage, causation, and fault/negligence.

#### 4. CL.CACH.Art.133 — Denegación de Embarque (Código Aeronáutico)

**Verbatim Text (BCN):** "Artículo 133.- Denegación de Embarque. En el evento que el transportador prevea que tendrá que denegar el embarque de uno o más pasajeros **por sobreventa**, los cuales se hubieren presentado oportunamente y cuyo billete de pasaje estuviere previamente confirmado en un vuelo determinado, deberá pedir en primer lugar que se presenten voluntarios..."

**Agent-Fit:** Air carrier (transportador).

**Applicability to CL-001:**
The violation's own history note correctly identifies the **critical limitation**: Art. 133 applies specifically to denied boarding **due to overbooking (sobreventa)**. The removal of the passenger from flight LA8159 was predicated on a **false accusation of aggression**, not overbooking. Therefore, **Art. 133 is not the applicable provision** for this removal.

**What Art. 133 Does Provide:** Even though the article is inapplicable to the removal itself, it establishes the **regulatory framework** for passenger rights in denied boarding scenarios. If the carrier's conduct is later characterized as a de facto denied boarding (even without overbooking), the statutory options (volunteers, compensation, re-routing, refund) in Arts. 133 A–133 E provide useful benchmarks for what the carrier **should have done** — and what it failed to do. The violation's history note accurately states: "The carrier failed to provide any of the statutory options or compensation required under Art. 133, and instead used the false accusation as the basis for a lifetime ban."

**Correct Legal Basis for the Removal:** The removal should be analyzed under:
- The **air transport contract** (Código Civil and consumer law), for breach without lawful cause.
- **LPDC Art. 12** (failure to honour agreed conditions) and **Art. 3.b** (right to truthful information).
- Potentially **CACH Art. 131 bis** (carrier's right to refuse transport on security grounds), but only if the security ground was genuine. PDI's exoneration undermines any such justification.

**Verdict:** **Provision is correct as cited, but inapplicable to the specific facts of CL-001.** The article number and text are accurate, but the factual predicate (sobreventa) is absent. The candidate should be retained for the regulatory framework it provides, but the primary legal basis for the removal lies in contract and consumer law.
