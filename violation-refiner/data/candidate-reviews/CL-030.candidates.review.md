### Key Takeaways

1.  **Established articles (Arts. 255 CP, 3.b and 23 LPDC)** are correctly identified and properly supported by the verbatim BCN text. The legacy paraphrases for Art. 255 CP should be updated to the current wording ("desempeñando un acto del servicio" rather than "abusando de su oficio").

2.  **Art. 223 and Art. 224 CP** are **not viable** for the DGAC or PDI conduct described. They are *prevaricación judicial*, aimed exclusively at judges and judicial prosecutors. Any attempt to frame administrative prevarication against PDI/DGAC officials must rely on other provisions (e.g., Art. 255 CP for vejaciones, or Art. 256 CP for malicious refusal to provide protection/service).

3.  **Art. 269 ter CP** is **viable only against the PDI officer**, not the DGAC official. The article explicitly names "el funcionario policial" as a possible active subject. If the PDI officer knowingly omitted the exoneration finding from the official *parte* or failed to preserve CCTV evidence, this article could apply.

4.  The "check failed — Not Found" flags in the original file for the candidates are accurate: the article numbers as cited either do not exist as separate administrative‑prevarication provisions (223, 224) or require a different agent class (269 ter).
